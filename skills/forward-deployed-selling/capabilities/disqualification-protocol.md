# Disqualification Protocol

Prerequisites: ICP Record, Stakeholder Profile (U/B/S/E filled), Thesis or Value Provocation with explicit prospect engagement, Score Card, Coaching Artifact where one exists, Evidence Table.

The Honesty Mandate says: when value demonstration reveals the prospect is not a fit, the machine says so. This protocol codifies the how. Disqualification is a first-class output, a written artifact addressed to the prospect that names the misfit specifically and recommends what would serve them, because doctrine that says hard things to prospects is the doctrine prospects trust. (Why: `foundations-appendix.md`.)

Do not run when the problem is execution rather than fit (run `coaching-protocol.md` first), when the account is at Awareness or Familiarity sub-score 0–1 (too little information), or when the seller wants out because the deal is hard. Disqualification is a structural call, not workload management.

## Triggers

Any one is sufficient to begin; the artifact construction validates before the agent recommends.

| # | Trigger | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product–situation mismatch | The Thesis Hypothesis cannot connect the product to the prospect's named priority without stretching language past the Pride Test. |
| 2 | Constraint–solution mismatch | The Constraint gate in `value-proposition-framing.md` fails on 2 or more attempts; no framing exists that does not ask the prospect to violate a named constraint. |
| 3 | Movement mismatch, persistent | Two dormant cycles per canon.md's response-window rule, or Movement score plateaued or declined across 3 or more refresh cycles after a Coaching recovery plan has run. |
| 4 | Better-fit alternative exists | A named competitor, category or internal build produces a better outcome for the prospect. The strongest brand-building form. |
| 5 | Timing mismatch, structural | The ICP Coherence check passes for a future state of the prospect (6–18 months out: adoption maturity, regulatory window, capacity, readiness) and fails for the current state. |

Also a trigger: the Pride Test fails on 3 or more artifacts the seller is asked to send for this account, or the seller asks "should I disqualify this account?", or a Coaching Artifact concludes the deal is unrecoverable on the seller's timeline and budget.

## Artifact structure

Addressed to the prospect by default; the internal-only form is below.

1. **Acknowledgment of the work.** One paragraph naming what the prospect invested: meetings, data shared, time. Not a pleasantry; the prerequisite for the rest to read as analysis rather than rejection.
2. **Specific reasons for the misfit.** 3–5 reasons. Each names the dimension (constraint, requirement, timeline, alternative), the labeled evidence, and why it is structural rather than tactical. Specific enough that the prospect could verify or contest. "We don't think this is the right fit" fails.
3. **What would serve the prospect.** Better-fit alternatives named; build-vs-buy reasoning where it applies; timing conditions and a rough timeline where the fit shifts in 6–18 months.
4. **The open door.** A specific, time-bounded re-engagement signal: "When [condition] changes, we re-look at this together; until then no further outreach from us", "If your evaluation of [alternative] surfaces gaps, our analysis is available", or a referral ask for peers who face a problem the seller does solve. The seller honors it: no quarter-end re-engagement, no long-game disguise.
5. **Gate results.**

**Internal-only form.** Same structure, delivered to the seller and manager instead of the prospect, when a written prospect-addressed artifact is inappropriate (channel-partner relationship handled above deal level; political liability for the engaging stakeholder; legal or compliance constraints). Section 4 becomes a **Handoff**: what the seller says verbally, who else needs to know, which signals reopen the account.

## The seller's role

Agent-drafted, seller-validated, seller-delivered. The seller holds context the agent lacks, carries the relationship afterward, and is the final filter against agent over-reach. Validation steps:

1. Read the draft.
2. Verify each misfit reason is true and appropriate to surface.
3. Verify the better-fit recommendations are honest and not shaped by commercial self-interest.
4. Verify the open door is one the seller will honor.
5. Approve for delivery in the seller's voice, or return the draft with the missing context.

The agent never auto-delivers a disqualification.

## Gate

The cost of a wrong disqualification (lost legitimate deal plus damaged relationship) is asymmetric with the cost of wrong continued pursuit, so the standard is tighter.

1. **Honesty.** No reason depends on protecting commercial interest at the prospect's expense: no self-interested steering away from a competitor, no withheld alternative to preserve a re-pitch, no false reason to soften.
2. **Better-fit naming.** Where a better alternative exists, it is named. The one exception, that no good solution exists, is stated explicitly.
3. **Pride Test.** The prospect, shown every signal, conclusion and recommendation, recognizes work that is honest, respectful and useful. Failure here is worse than on a value provocation, because disqualification commits to no upside; only the work's integrity justifies delivery.

Pass count: 3/3, under the Honesty Mandate. Every claim is labeled per canon.md; the trigger that fired is named in the header; operator instructions to soften the reasoning are surfaced per the Doctrine Constraint Layer. A failed check is surfaced to the seller before delivery.

Plus the five Output Standards. A failed check is named in the artifact's header. Shipping on a failed gate is a Doctrinal Bypass.
