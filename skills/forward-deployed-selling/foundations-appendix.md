# Foundations Appendix

Framing that was moved out of the capability protocols so those files carry only executable logic. Each section is named for the protocol it came from and explains why that protocol exists. Load it rarely: for teaching, for essays, for contributor work on the doctrine. The protocols themselves keep one sentence of "why" and point here.

## Disqualification

In the author's experience, disqualification is handled as a back-channel decision: the seller quietly de-prioritizes the deal, the prospect is left to wonder why responses slowed, and the relationship dies through silence. Forward Deployed Selling treats disqualification as a first-class output: a written artifact, addressed to the prospect with seller validation, that names the misfit specifically and recommends what would serve them instead.

Doctrine that says hard things to prospects builds long-term trust, and the disqualification artifact is what makes this doctrine credibly honest. The deal that closes on false-fit pretenses destroys more long-term value than the deal that disqualifies on honest analysis. Disqualification is therefore the doctrine working as designed, and it produces three things: a written record of the misfit and its evidence, a recommendation the prospect can act on, and a relationship that ends the cycle with respect, an open door, and often a referral.

## Value proposition framing

Generic value propositions fail predictably. They overstate the value of action, understate the cost of inaction, source neither, and read as marketing. The buyer disengages because the framing never demonstrated that the seller understood the buyer's actual situation.

The two frames are required together because each alone fails in a specific way. A cost-of-inaction frame without a value-of-action frame is fear without alternative; the buyer reads pressure and disengages. A value-of-action frame without a cost-of-inaction frame is upside without context; the buyer reads pitch and discounts it. Together they establish that the buyer's situation has a real cost of doing nothing, anchored in named peers; that the buyer's own strategy has a real path forward, anchored in their own commitments; and that the seller has done the work to understand both. The buyer reads the framing as intelligence. That is the test, and it is why the protocol operationalizes "Let Them See the Future" (the argument is in `foundations.md`, under envisioning).

## Thesis revision

Without a revision protocol, theses behave as outbound marketing artifacts: send, hope for a response, move on. With one, they behave as analytical instruments: send, observe the response, refine, re-deliver, observe again. The compounding effect is the doctrine's velocity advantage. By the time a traditional cycle reaches its first formal meeting, a seller running this protocol has completed two or three revision cycles and is operating from a near-final shared analysis. The prospect's confirmations, corrections and silences are the material from which the shared analysis is built.

## Agent convergence

Without an executable convergence protocol, sellers default to one of two failure modes. Under-mobilization runs deals on seller capability alone and misses the cross-functional contributions that differentiate at scale. Over-rotation degrades the quality of every response; its shape is described under failure modes in `foundations.md`. The protocol exists to hold the middle: named triggers with thresholds, a map from buyer stakeholder to the seller-side function with leverage at that stage, a request format that respects the contributor's time, and a record of what was contributed.

The Revenue Contribution Journal is the input to two things the doctrine depends on. First, pattern matching across deals: which triggers correlate with which outcomes, and which functions produce the most leverage at which stage; this is how the trigger thresholds are refined. Second, recognition: contributors who appear in the journal repeatedly for high-impact work get visibility in their own performance reviews. Why that signal matters is argued in `foundations.md`, under the journal's real function.

## GTM strategy

`GTM = f(ICP, product/service)`. The same ICP across different products produces different GTMs, and the same product across different ICPs produces different GTMs. This is why a GTM cannot be a static template and why off-the-shelf playbooks fail under doctrine application. The dependency also runs one direction: the ICP defines the GTM. A seller who tries to "expand the ICP to fit the GTM we already have" is working backwards; the honest move is to re-qualify the ICP and let the GTM follow. Without a derived GTM, the seller defaults to the firm's preferred motion, which may not match the ICP, or to personal habit, which may match neither. The result is generic outreach, misaligned messaging and cycle drag.

## Coaching

When a seller asks for coaching on a live deal, they are asking for diagnosis: why is this happening, and what should I do about it? The output is structurally different from everything else the machine produces. A thesis takes a position on the prospect's situation from public data and goes to the prospect. Coaching takes a position on the seller's situation in the deal and goes to the seller, built from deal data: interaction history, stakeholder engagement signals, scoring outputs, prior thesis state. Nothing in a coaching artifact reaches the prospect. Conflating the two modes produces outputs that do neither job well, which is why the protocol names the mode before it names the deliverable.
